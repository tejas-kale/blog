# Squad stability and league performance

There is a published literature on squad stability and league performance. None of it uses this analysis’s measure: the median current senior-squad streak of regulars, then the within-club correlation of that median with results points.

The nearest published papers measure something else, usually how much of the squad is new from one season to the next, or how long teammates have shared a club.

## Published journal articles

- Fabrizio Montanari, Giacomo Silvestri, and Edoardo Gallo, “Team Performance between Change and Stability: The Case of the Italian ‘Serie A’,” *Journal of Sport Management* 22, no. 6 (2008): 701–716, [https://doi.org/10.1123/jsm.22.6.701](https://doi.org/10.1123/jsm.22.6.701). All players at 35 Serie A clubs, 1994–95 through 2002–03. Stability and the longevity of relationships among teammates are associated with better performance. They also report weak evidence that stability helps only up to a point.
- Vicenç Fernandez, Pep Simo, and Jose M. Sallan, “Turnover and balance between exploration and exploitation processes for high-performance teams,” *Team Performance Management* 22, no. 3/4 (2016): 204–222, [https://doi.org/10.1108/TPM-08-2015-0035](https://doi.org/10.1108/TPM-08-2015-0035). One club, Manchester United, over 24 seasons ending in 2008–09. They report an inverted U: some turnover is associated with better performance, and both very low and very high turnover are worse, depending on how turnover is counted.

## Premier League manuscript that does not appear to have been published

Mark van Vugt, Claire Hart, and Tirza Leader wrote “Does Stability Foster Team Performance? A European Football (Soccer) Inquiry.” Van Vugt’s own publication list still describes it as a manuscript submitted for publication (2008): [https://professormarkvanvugt.com/publications/articles/applications/159-does-stability-foster-team-performance-2008](https://professormarkvanvugt.com/publications/articles/applications/159-does-stability-foster-team-performance-2008). The draft covers nine seasons of the Premier League and Serie A and reports that more stable squads finished with more points, a better rank, more goals scored, and fewer conceded, after allowing for past performance, wealth, contract length, and age. The PDF is on his site: [https://www.professormarkvanvugt.com/images/files/DoesStabilityFosterTeamPerformance.pdf](https://www.professormarkvanvugt.com/images/files/DoesStabilityFosterTeamPerformance.pdf).

## Theses and observatory reports

- Stefan Göke, *Personnel Turnover and the Dynamics of Team Performance: Evidence from German Association Football* (PhD diss., Universität Paderborn, 2012), [https://d-nb.info/1036551814/34](https://d-nb.info/1036551814/34). In the Bundesliga, sides with more stable rosters outperform sides with high turnover, and losing team-specific experience hurts even when the replacement has similar market value.
- Pedro Pires, “The Impact of Player Turnover on Football Teams” (master’s thesis, Instituto Superior Técnico, Lisbon), [https://fenix.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/downloadFile/1970719973968546/Tese_PedroPires_84687.pdf](https://fenix.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/downloadFile/1970719973968546/Tese_PedroPires_84687.pdf). Across several leagues, clubs that field fewer new players are more likely to gain points relative to the previous season.
- CIES Football Observatory, Monthly Report 34 (April 2018), “The importance of squad stability,” [https://football-observatory.com/IMG/pdf/mr34en.pdf](https://football-observatory.com/IMG/pdf/mr34en.pdf), and Monthly Report 38, on player turnover in the big five leagues, [https://football-observatory.com/IMG/sites/mr/mr38/en/](https://football-observatory.com/IMG/sites/mr/mr38/en/). These are research notes. They find that higher-finishing clubs have a smaller share of new signings, and that clubs fielding more players earn fewer points per match.

## What this analysis asks instead

Those studies mostly compare clubs with one another in a given window, using the share of new players. This analysis asks a different question: inside one club, across its own seasons, does a longer current streak among regulars line up with more points? The published work generally finds that more stable squads collect more points. That result can sit next to a within-club median near zero, because a rich club can be both stable and high-scoring without its own quieter seasons being its higher-scoring ones.
