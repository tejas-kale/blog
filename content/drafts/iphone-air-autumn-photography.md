This is a teachable, four-week course, not a finished essay. Follow it in order. Each week has a learning goal, a short lesson, a neighbourhood assignment, and a cull-and-edit ritual. The phone is an **iPhone Air**: one 48MP Fusion rear camera (26 mm, ƒ/1.6), optical-quality 2× at 52 mm, no ultra-wide, no dedicated telephoto, no macro, no ProRAW. Research notes and sources live in `research/iphone-air-autumn-nature.md`.

**Outcome after four weeks:** a small set of photographs made within walking distance of home, lightly edited in Photos, ready to publish as a short series on this blog. Peak Berlin colour is usually mid to late October; start the clock so week 3–4 land in that window if you can.

**Rules of the project**

- Shoot only with the stock Camera app. Edit only in Photos, and only a little.
- Stay in the neighbourhood on weekdays. One optional longer walk on a weekend is enough.
- Publish process, not perfection: what you tried, what failed, which frame you kept.
- Do not invent autumn with sliders. If the tree is still green, photograph green.

## How this course is structured

| Week | Skill | Neighbourhood assignment | Blog output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 (one evening) | Configure the phone | Test shots of the same tree at 1× / 28 mm / 35 mm / 2× | Settings appendix in post 1 |
| 1 | Light and exposure | Same corner at three times of day | Post: light |
| 2 | Focal length and composition | Walk 20 minutes; 12 frames, twelve different compositions | Post: seeing |
| 3 | Subject at two scales | One wide scene, one tight detail, same subject | Post: scale |
| 4 | Sequence, cull, publish | A 6–8 image edit from the month | Post: the set |

Success is not “stunning” as a mood. It is: horizons level, highlights not clipped on bright leaves, subject unambiguous, and a caption that says where and when.

## Week 0 — Configure the Air once

### Formats

Settings → Camera → Formats:

- **High Efficiency** (HEIF). Required if you want to change Photographic Styles later in Photos.
- Turn on **Resolution Control** so the Camera app can show 24MP / 48MP.
- Leave the default at **24MP**. That is Apple’s Fusion file: detail plus HDR, and it still records depth so you can add a portrait blur later. Use 48MP only for a static landscape you expect to crop. Night mode, Live Photos, flash, 2×, and Portrait will not give you a 48MP depth-rich file.

Settings → Camera → Preserve Settings: enable **Exposure Adjustment** and **Resolution Control** so the app does not silently reset.

### Composition aids

Settings → Camera: **Grid** and **Level** on. The grid is for thirds and verticals (façades, trunks). The level is for horizons and canal edges.

Settings → Camera → Photographic Styles: start on **Standard**. Skip **Bright** for foliage; it is tuned to lift skin and add global pop.

Optional: set the **Action button** to Camera, and learn the **Camera Control** (lower-right edge): press to open or shoot, light double-press to cycle Exposure / Zoom / Styles / Tone. Sliding on the Control changes the selected parameter without jogging the phone the way a pinch-zoom does.

### Focal lengths you actually have

Think in four honest framings, all from the same sensor:

1. **1× / 26 mm** — default. Environment plus subject. Easy to include too much pavement.
2. **28 mm and 35 mm** — Settings → Camera → Customise Main Camera (wording varies slightly by iOS). These are crops. Use them as a composition discipline: they force you to decide what the picture is about before you press the shutter.
3. **2× / 52 mm** — optical-quality crop. Best “portrait of a tree,” compressed street-tree-against-façade, a single leaf you cannot physically approach.
4. **Digital zoom past 2×** — for scouting only. If the picture only works at 5×, walk closer or give up.

**Panorama** (up to 63MP) is your ultra-wide. Rotate from the hips with locked elbows. Use it for a canal, an avenue, or a courtyard you cannot step back from. Do not panorama a scene with moving trams unless you want stitches.

### Assignment 0

Tonight, photograph **one tree you will reuse all month**. Four frames: 1×, 28 mm, 35 mm, 2×, same standpoint. No editing. Look at them on a larger screen. Notice how 26 mm includes parked cars and sky, and 52 mm turns the tree into a subject. Keep this tree as a control so later weeks are comparable.

## Week 1 — Light is the subject

Autumn in Berlin is often overcast. That is an advantage: clouds are a giant softbox, so yellows and reds record without the harsh specular glare of July sun. What fails on phones is **the bright leaf against a darker trunk** — Smart HDR 5 tries to hold both, and the leaf goes creamy.

### Practice

1. Open Camera, Photo mode, 1×, Standard style, Live Photos off if you want the option of 48MP later (for week 1, 24MP is fine).
2. Tap the **brightest leaves** you care about. Drag the exposure sun **down** until those leaves have texture, even if the shadows go a bit dark. You can lift shadows 5–10 points in Photos. You cannot restore a clipped yellow.
3. For mixed sun and shade, touch-and-hold until **AE/AF Lock** appears, recompose, then nudge exposure.
4. Backlight: stand so the sun is behind the canopy. Expose for the glow in the leaves, not the sky. The sky may go pale; that is acceptable. A silhouette of trunks against colour is a valid picture.
5. Golden hour here in October is short and low. Side light rakes texture on bark and cobbles. Front light (sun behind you) is the most boring; walk until the light is beside or behind the subject.
6. Blue hour and wet streets: let Night mode appear. Hold still; the shutter is long. Rest the Air against a pole if needed. Do not expect 48MP.

Weather to chase on purpose: high cloud, fog, rain just after it stops (puddles), a hard freeze if it comes (geometry of bare twigs). Do not wait for “perfect sunset.”

### Assignment 1

Pick **one street corner** within ten minutes of home. Photograph it on three visits: morning commute light, overcast midday, and the last 30 minutes before sunset. Same standpoint, same 1× framing, plus one extra frame each visit at 2×. Keep six photos.

**Edit (cap: two minutes per image):** straighten; if the sky is empty and bright, crop it down rather than crushing highlights; Highlights −10 to −25 if leaves are hot; Shadows +5 to +15 in the dusk set; warmth only if the file looks colder than the scene you remember. Stop.

**Publish this week:** a short post with three images (one per visit) and a paragraph on which light made the colour believable. Include EXIF time of day if you like (Photos → ⓘ).

## Week 2 — Composition without an ultra-wide

Phone landscapes fail because 26 mm from standing eye-height is a document of a pavement. You have to **choose a foreground** or **get higher/lower**.

### Tools

- **Foreground:** a leaf on cobble, a railing, a bicycle wheel, a puddle. Put it on a lower third; put the tree or façade on an upper third. The grid is there for this.
- **Frame in a frame:** Hinterhof arch, tram shelter, gap between parked vans. 1× loves this; 2× often kills the frame.
- **Verticals:** Berlin façades punish a tilted camera. Use the level. Correct remaining keystone with Photos → Crop → the vertical/horizontal perspective sliders, **lightly**.
- **Leading lines:** canal edge, tram track, wet kerb. Start the line in a corner, not dead centre, unless the symmetry is the joke.
- **One subject.** If you cannot name it in five words (“maple against yellow Plattenbau”), you have not framed yet.

Move your feet instead of zooming. The Air’s 2× is a compositional lens, not binoculars.

### Assignment 2

A 20-minute loop you already walk. **Twelve frames, twelve different ideas**, not twelve nearly identical trees:

- Rule of thirds
- Dead-centre symmetry (a path, a courtyard)
- Low (phone near the leaf litter)
- High (steps, a bridge, a U-Bahn exit)
- Through something (fence, branches)
- Reflection
- Detail that is not a leaf (bark, seed, snail, rust)
- A person-scale figure for size, if they are willing, or a bicycle
- Tight 2×
- Deliberately empty (fog, sky, one bench)
- Colour against grey (one red leaf, concrete)
- Panorama of a space you cannot step back from

Cull to **four**. Editing as week 1, plus crop only when the composition is 10% off — do not crop a confused picture into a good one.

**Publish:** four images, each with one sentence naming the compositional idea.

## Week 3 — Two scales, and the close-up you can actually get

Without macro, “close-up” means **the closest distance at which AF locks**, then 2× if you still need tightness. If the focus square pulses, you are too close. The Air’s portraits (next-generation Focus Control) work on more than faces: a tree, a teapot-sized subject in dedicated Portrait mode, or a Photo-mode shot that recorded depth (24MP, not 48MP). Use blur only when it **separates** a subject from a busy playground. If the background is already a wash of colour, skip Portrait; fake-bokeh on foliage looks like a smear.

### Two-scale method

1. Photograph the **place** (bench + tree + block).
2. Without walking more than a few metres, photograph **one object** that belongs to that place (a specific leaf, a mushroom after rain, moss on a Hinterhof wall).
3. The pair should feel like they were made together. Same light, same white balance (Standard style, no sudden Bright).

Water: canals and puddles double the world and give you width the lens does not have. Expose for the highlight in the reflection.

Wind: leaves move. Burst (drag shutter left, or hold volume up) and pick the sharp frame.

### Assignment 3

One subject, two scales, on **three different days** (so nine frames before cull). Weather should differ if possible. Cull to one pair you would hang as a diptych, plus one spare.

**Publish:** the diptych and a note on minimum focus distance — how close was too close.

## Week 4 — Cull like an editor, edit like a coward, publish like a writer

You now have a pile. Most of it should be deleted.

### Cull

On the phone the same evening:

1. Delete obvious misses (blur, clipped leaves, thumb).
2. For each location, keep **one** hero, not five variants.
3. Keep the week-0 control tree if it shows seasonal change.

A publishable month is **6–8 photographs**, not 40. Transfer full-resolution keepers off the phone if storage matters; the blog never gets those files.

### Edit budget (Photos)

Per image, in this order:

1. Crop / straighten / tiny perspective
2. Exposure if the whole file is dark (dusk)
3. Highlights down, shadows up — small
4. Photographic Style: warmer *or* more intense, not both at the stops
5. Brilliance / Vibrance a little; Saturation almost never
6. Sharpness/Definition only if the file will be shown small on the web
7. Compare with Original. If you cannot tell they are the same walk, you over-edited.

Do not: orange-shift greens, add a heavy vignette, sky-replace, or Clean Up stray bins as a habit. HEIF from this camera is already processed.

### Export for Hugo

- Share → Save to Files as JPEG, or duplicate and export.
- Long edge about **1600–2500 px**. 48MP originals are the wrong artefact for GitHub Pages.
- Filename: `2026-10-12-landwehrkanal-1x.jpg` style, dated.
- Put files in `static/photos/autumn-2026/` and reference them with the existing `relURL` shortcode pattern used for charts.

### Assignment 4

Build **one set of 6–8 images** that a stranger could read as a place and a season. Write 400–800 words: where the loop is (enough to be honest, not enough to invite strangers to your door), what the Air cannot do, and what walking the same blocks taught you. That is the capstone post. Earlier weekly posts can stay short.

## Field checklist (screenshot this)

- Grid and level on; style Standard; 24MP HEIF
- 1× unless you have a reason for 2×
- Tap the **highlight** you need to keep; expose down
- AE/AF Lock when you recompose
- Clean the lens (jacket pockets)
- Hold the phone with elbows in; Camera Control instead of pinch-zoom
- If AF hunts, step back
- After rain, look down (puddles) and up (clearing sky through canopy)
- Cull the same day
- Two-minute edit, then stop

## Common failures (and the fix)

| Failure | Cause on the Air | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creamy yellow blobs | Exposed for the trunk/sky | Meter the leaf, lower exposure |
| “Nice park” but no picture | 26 mm from standing height | Foreground or 2×, or kneel |
| Mushy zoom | Digital zoom | 2× maximum, then walk |
| Soft close-up | Inside minimum focus, or motion | Step back, burst, brace |
| Neon autumn | Saturation + fake warmth | Undo; trust overcast colour |
| Leaning buildings | Handheld wide | Level; slight perspective crop |
| HDR halo on branches | Fighting a bright sky | Change standpoint, include less sky, or accept a pale sky |
| Night-mode mush | Handheld 2 s | Brace, or come back |

## What “done” looks like

You can explain, without looking this up: why 24MP is the default on this phone; when 2× is honest; how to protect yellow highlights; why panorama exists on a one-camera phone; and you have published at least three posts with photographs you made on repeated neighbourhood walks, edited only in Photos.

If a week is rainy, that week’s lesson is weather, not a skip.
