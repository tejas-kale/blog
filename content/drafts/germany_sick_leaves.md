BBC More or Less' recent [podcast episode](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sounds/play/w3ct9995) on the statistic that employees in German take roughly 19.5 days of sick leave every year was interesting. It told that the number comes from the statutory insurer DAK's [report](https://www.dak.de/dak/unternehmen/reporte-forschung/gesundheitsreport-2026_223108), which counts all calendar days (including weekends) and excludes child-sickness and maternity leaves. Given that Germany has 93 statutory health insurers, and the claim from an expert in the episode that the average is skewed by a handful of long-term absences, I wanted to answer two questions:

1. How representative are the people insured by DAK for the whole of Germany?
2. How skewed is the headline statistic of 19.5, and what is the median number of sick days taken by employees in Germany?

To answer the first question, we can look at a similar [report](https://www.tk.de/resource/blob/2214302/9c99dbb2cea6422e50c6009c50e57e37/gesundheitsreport-au-2026-data.pdf) published by another statutory insurer, Techniker Krankenkasse (TK), which is quite popular among the immigrant and tech community in Germany. TK is larger than DAK with ~12.3 million people insured compared to DAK's 5.4 million, or roughly 16% versus 7% of the statutory system. Their study estimates the average sick leave per year from their insurees at 18.5 days. Thus, while not conclusive, the TK report provides further evidence that the DAK mean is not a quirk of one fund.

Unfortunately, neither report publishes the median sick days per person per year. But it is instructive to look at DAK's data grouped by the duration of each absence. As seen from the chart below, absences greater than 6 weeks account for only 2.5% of sick absences but 37.9% of sick days. Conversely, 40.1% of absences last 1–3 days but account for only 8.3% of sick days. 

![Long absences are rare but dominate total sick days]({{< relURL "charts/dak-absence-duration.png" >}})

DAK's report also states that 37.5% of its members had no recorded absences. It means that while these people perhaps were absent from work due to sickness during the year, the duration of each of their absences was fewer than 4 days and they did not visit a doctor during the sickness. Among the remaining 62.5%, there were 2 episodes of absence each year on average and the mean duration of each absence was ~9.8 days.

After hearing the podcast, my first interpretation was that a regular employee in Germany takes nearly 3 weeks of sick leaves each year which did not correlate with my own personal experience or that of my peers. But after peering into the data, I realise that the situation is more complex. As evident from the chart above, the average values reported are skewed significantly by the longer absences. If only they had reported some median values!
