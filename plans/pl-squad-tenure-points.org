* Aim and intended use

- Question :: Across Premier League club-seasons from 1995–96 through 2025–26, do clubs with a longer current senior-squad streak finish on more points?
- Intended use :: A short public blog post, published whether the association is strong, weak, or absent.
- Audience :: Readers of the blog who know a Premier League table and can read a correlation.
- Population :: Club-seasons in the 31 completed 38-game Premier League seasons from 1995–96 through 2025–26.
- Decision informed :: Which regular-player cut, if either, the post shows, and how the result is described.
- This analysis cannot support :: A claim that time together causes points. Money, the manager, injuries, and the schedule remain mixed in with tenure.

* Risk tier

- Tier :: production-or-external
- Reason :: The result is published. A reader can treat the chart as a finding about Premier League squads. The run keeps a saved copy of the retrieved tables, recomputes points from a second source, and checks one player's tenure by hand.

* Method and assumptions

- Method class :: descriptive
- Method :: For each club-season, take the regulars under one cut and compute the median of their current senior-squad tenure. For each club, compute the Pearson correlation between that median and results points across its seasons in the window. The lead number is the median of those club correlations. A 10% trimmed mean of the same correlations is computed beside it. Spearman is reported as a point value and is not bootstrapped.
- Assumptions :: A season in the senior squad counts as shared time with that club's group, including a season with no appearances and a season outside the Premier League. A full season on loan breaks that shared time. Results points measure the season's on-pitch outcome. The clubs in the window are the whole population, so the bootstrap redraws seasons inside each club.
- Limits :: The association is descriptive. Two regular cuts are both computed. The post shows starts, appearances, or both only after Tejas chooses. The agent does not choose the chart.

Tenure rules the code must implement:

- A season adds 1 when the player was in that club's senior squad, in any division, including a season with zero appearances.
- Years only in the academy add nothing and leave the streak unchanged.
- A full season on loan means the player is absent from the parent senior squad for that whole season. The parent gets 0. The earlier run is wiped. A later return starts at 1.
- A mid-season loan means the player is in the parent senior squad and in another club's senior squad in the same season. Each of those clubs gets 0.5. The parent streak stays in place. The next full season at the parent adds 1 on top of the 0.5.
- A mid-season permanent move adds 0.5 at the club they left and 0.5 at the club they joined. The streak at the club they left ends. A later return there starts at 1.
- If one season lists two loan clubs, the parent gets 0.5 once and each loan club gets 0.5.
- The same tenure is used for both regular cuts. The cuts differ only in who enters the median.

Uncertainty:

- 2000 replicates, seed 2026.
- Each replicate redraws a club's seasons with replacement and keeps that season's tenure and points together.
- It recomputes each club correlation, then the median across clubs.
- The 95% interval is the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles.
- A replicate that makes a club's tenure constant leaves that club out of that replicate's median.
- Each plotted club's interval is the percentiles of its own recomputed correlation.

Summary rule:

- The post leads with the median of defined club correlations.
- Clubs with undefined correlations are excluded from the median and from the trimmed mean, and they are named.
- The trimmed mean drops the lowest 10% and the highest 10% of defined correlations, then takes the mean. It lives in the notebook.
- When undefined clubs are more than 20% of clubs with at least 10 seasons, the opening paragraph of the post states that share.

* Decision Ledger

| Decision | Reason | Alternatives rejected | Consequence |
|----------+--------+-----------------------+-------------|
| Describe the association | The blog can show whether tenure and points move together | A causal claim about time together | The post states the limit in the Aim section |
| Results points only | On-pitch outcomes are wins and draws | Official table points; goals scored | Deductions appear in a note, not in the plotted total |
| 38-game seasons only | Raw points then share one scale, and the window is already 31 seasons | Points per game across 42-game seasons | 1992–93 through 1994–95 stay out |
| Within-club correlations | The question is whether a club's own settled seasons coincide with its own points | One pooled cloud of every club-season | Rich settled clubs do not set the single slope |
| Median tenure of regulars | Agreed exposure | Pairwise shared seasons | Pairwise overlap is out of this post |
| Senior-squad streak, with the loan and move rules above | Tenure should follow time at the club, and a full loan breaks it | Cumulative Premier League appearances only; academy years | Tenure can rise during a Championship season |
| Both regular cuts computed, chart chosen later | Starts and appearances can disagree | Publishing both, or one, before seeing them | Tejas judges the post after both results exist |
| Median is always the lead number | The median already resists edge clubs. Undefined clubs are already removed from both summaries | Switching the lead number to a trimmed mean when the drop rate passes 20% or 40% | A drop rate above 20% changes the opening sentence, not the statistic |
| R and =worldfootballR= | One maintained package reads FBref and Transfermarkt | Python =soccerdata=, which does not read Transfermarkt | The CRAN package is not the install |
| Org tables resolve identity | Automatic name matching will be wrong in places Tejas can see | Letting the agent pick among ambiguous ids | Unresolved club-seasons stay out of the correlation |
| Bootstrap seasons inside clubs | These clubs are the population | Resampling clubs as if they were a sample | The interval is season-to-season uncertainty |
